# Shure-SM57-Repair

## Behavior before repair
- The microphone didn't have an output
- The top of it with the little mesh as pop filter was spinning like crazy
- It looked great otherwise

## Disassembly
The first thing I had to do was to disassemble the entire thing. There are plenty of videos of how to do this thing and I strongly recommend anyone who is going to repair or take apart the microphone to watch one, because the internals are really delicate. Especially the diaphragm with coil.
The microphone is composed of two main parts. The bottom with the XLR connector and the top part or body with the cartridge inside. The bottom part can be easily unscrewed but since our problem is not in the connection to the connector itself, we leave it as it is. The top part is more painful and I'm not going to explain how you should disassemble it. Just look at the tutorial.

## The problem and it's repair (not really)
when i first opened the cartridge, the thin diaphragm with its little coil was falling off. It was entirely separated from the body with magnet so I had to be careful.

*here is how the diaphragm looks*

<img width=30% height=auto alt="Diaphragm" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8e652372-cebc-4b94-9ed1-cf8eed4c4e90" />

as you can see, besides being completely detached from the body, the diaphragm itself looks in great condition

Then I scraped the old glue off of the cartridge body. It's important to note that any of that "dust" you scrape off of the body doesn't fall into the magnet before reattaching and glueing the coil or otherwise the coil will rub against the magnet.

<img width=30% height=auto alt="Body and cartridge" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ee822167-6c23-455d-9ead-7928552feb3c" />


Speaking of this, another tough thing you have to do is getting the coil somehow exactly in the middle of the magnet, which isn't an easy task either. Next i glued back the diaphragm to the body using super glue which is **wrong** and you should absolutely not do the same thing. While the super glue holds it really well, it  also works as an insulator and when you look back at the diaphragm, it has a kind of copper mesh around the coil which works as shielding. Without proper connection to the ground, it cannot work as shielding and becomes more of an antenna that picks up noise and unwanted buzz. **You need to use conductive glue!**

#### The broken wire (the culprit)
The last thing and maybe the hardest one is glueing back the extremely thin diaphragm wire.
